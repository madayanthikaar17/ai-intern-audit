================================================================================
A2 CONCEPTUAL EXPERIMENT: LINE-WEIGHTED VS AGGREGATE CORPUS FERTILITY
================================================================================

--- Part 1: Controlled 2-Line Example ---
Line 1:  2 words,  10 tokens -> fertility = 5.0
Line 2: 100 words, 100 tokens -> fertility = 1.0

Original line-level mean (line-weighted):
  (5.0 + 1.0) / 2 = 3.000000
Aggregate corpus fertility (word-weighted):
  (10 + 100) / (2 + 100) = 110 / 102 = 1.078431
Delta (Aggregate - Line-level):
  1.078431 - 3.000000 = -1.921569

Weighting comparison:
  Line-level mean : Line 1 = 50.00%, Line 2 = 50.00%
  Aggregate corpus: Line 1 = 1.96% of words, Line 2 = 98.04% of words

================================================================================
Summary of Conceptual Finding:
The original fertility.py calculates: mean(tokens_i / words_i) across lines.
This is mathematically valid and computes exactly what the script docstring states.
It is NOT an implementation bug. It is a conceptual metric distinction:
  - Line-weighted mean answers: 'What is the average fertility of a sentence?'
  - Aggregate fertility answers: 'What is the total token cost per word across the corpus?'
When sentences have unequal lengths, short outlier sentences exert disproportionate
influence on the line-weighted mean, shifting it away from the true corpus-wide cost.
